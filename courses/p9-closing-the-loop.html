Closing the loop

Until now Kittiwake Hall's twin has only talked. Tonight it is asked to touch the building: first by advising a person, then by drafting commands a person approves, and finally by writing setpoints itself, inside limits. Each step moves the person further from the radiator, so each step needs a reason to trust the twin and a way to stop it. You will weigh advice against control, price what being wrong costs in a building full of people, and write the guard that sits between a prediction and a command. Kittiwake Hall is a constructed six-storey teaching building; every number about it is generated by the bench engine.

Part 0

Kittiwake's twin, asked to act

The facilities team at Kittiwake Hall (constructed) liked the twin's setback proposal from practitioner session 7: lower the floors to 15 C at night and at weekends. Now someone asks the obvious next question. Why should a person retype a schedule the twin already wrote? Why not let the twin set the thermostats? The honest answer is "maybe, on some floors, inside limits, with a way back", and tonight is about each of those words.

LiveWhy "closing the loop" is a design decision, not a feature3 min▶

A twin that only reports is a dashboard with physics behind it. A twin whose predictions change what the building does has closed the loop, and from that moment its errors land on people. Every twin closes the loop somehow, even if the "actuator" is a facilities manager reading a weekly email. The design question is not whether, but through whom, within which limits, and with what record.

Real world

The National Academies (2024) report treats the twin as more than a model: it is updated from its physical counterpart and informs decisions that realize value. It calls "the bidirectional interaction between the virtual and the physical" central. That second direction, from twin back to asset, is the one this session takes seriously: a decision is where value is made, and where harm is done.

Self-studyWords for acting on a twin3 min▶
  • Advice. The twin proposes; a person decides and acts. The twin never touches a controller.
  • Approval. The twin drafts a specific command; a person approves or rejects each one before it is sent.
  • Control within limits. The twin writes setpoints itself, but only inside hard limits it cannot change.
  • Guardrail. A check between a prediction and a command that can refuse the command.
  • Fallback. The last schedule known to be safe, which the building returns to when the twin can no longer be trusted.
  • Override. A person's command that beats the twin's, always, and is logged like any other.
Part 1 · covers advice versus control and where the person stands

Advise, approve, or act within limits

8 min live

The same loop can be closed three ways. In each, the twin predicts and something changes in the building. What moves is the person: from the one who decides, to the one who signs, to the one who watches and can step in. The figure draws all three; only one of them lets the twin write to the building with no person in between.

LiveWhat each closure asks of the twin4 min▶

Advise. The twin proposes Kittiwake's setback schedule with its band, and a person decides. This only needs the twin to be useful on average and honest about its band: a bad proposal costs a person's attention, and the person is the guardrail. In practitioner session 8's ladder this is "advise, read-only", reachable after a check week.

Approve. The twin drafts one command at a time ("floor 3 to 15 C from 19:00") and a person signs each. Faster than advice, but a person who approves a command every few minutes soon stops reading them. Approval is only a guardrail while the volume is low enough to read.

Act within limits. The twin writes setpoints itself, but inside limits it cannot change: never below a comfort floor in occupied hours, never outside the range the plant was designed for, never on a floor whose surprises have shifted. This is the tier practitioner session 8 gated behind "the band held". The limits, not the model, are what you are really signing off.

LiveContested: when twins act without people2 min▶

Some researchers and practitioners describe "autonomous twins" that run whole buildings or plants with no person in the loop, and present that as the natural destination. Others argue that verification, validation and uncertainty quantification are not mature enough for that, that responsibility for a decision cannot be delegated to a model, and that most of the value comes from better advice anyway. There is no agreed timeline, and none is given here. What is not contested: the National Academies (2024) ask for validation and uncertainty work to be continual, adapting as the physical asset changes. Any twin that acts must keep earning it.

Self-studyApproval fatigue is a data problem2 min▶

Log every approval with the time it took and whether the command was changed. If approvals take seconds and nothing is ever changed, the person has become a rubber stamp and the tier is really "act" without the limits that "act" needs. Either reduce the volume (approve schedules, not single commands) or move to act-within-limits honestly, with the limits written down.

Part 2 · covers the cost of being wrong and an overconfident band

The cost of being wrong in a real building

8 min live

A forecasting error in a report is a number in a table. The same error, turned into a setpoint, is a seminar room at 09:00 full of people in coats. Wrong in one direction, people are cold now and nothing refunds it. Wrong in the other, the meter turns a little faster and next week's schedule can fix it. A guardrail should lean toward the cheaper mistake, and it can only do that if the band is honest.

LiveReading the guardrail calculator4 min▶

Cold surprises are rare, and they are the same for both settings. On floor 1 at the defaults, the reading fell more than 1 C below the twin's prediction in 1 of 50 occupied hours, whether the filter assumed honest sensor noise (0.4 C) or trusted the sensors fully (0.01 C). The predictions barely differ. What differs is how sure the twin says it is.

The band is where the danger hides. The honest band is about 2.19 C wide in occupied hours and held: the calculator prints the share of week-2 readings inside it. The overconfident band is about 1.26 C wide and fails the gate. A guardrail that trusts a too-narrow band sees a lower edge that looks comfortably high, and lets through commands it has not earned. Switch the comfort floor to 18 C and compare the refusals in the two rows.

At 19 C the guardrail refuses every occupied hour on floor 1, in both rows: 50 of 50. That is not a bug. In this constructed building the thermostat holds occupied rooms below 19 C (the calculator prints the twin's mean prediction), so a 19 C guardrail never lets the twin act. That is a finding a twin should surface before anyone automates: a guardrail that always refuses is a twin that only advises, and the limit, not the model, needs a conversation.

Toggle the unlogged retrofit. Floor 4's surprises shift (the bench's z of 11.44), so the state goes straight to fall back, whatever the band says. Notice the cold-surprise count: the retrofit made floor 4 warmer than the twin expects, so its surprises are warm. A detector that only watched for cold surprises would have stayed quiet.

LiveComfort, energy, equipment2 min▶

Three things can be harmed. Comfort: people in rooms, felt immediately, not recoverable. Energy: kWh on the meter, recoverable in the next schedule. Equipment: a plant cycled hard, a boiler asked for its full output every morning after a deep setback; slow, cumulative and often invisible to a room-temperature twin. Kittiwake's twin models rooms and mass, not boilers, so it has no right to act on equipment limits at all: those stay hard-coded. Energy costs money, and nothing here is investment advice: the energy and savings figures are constructed teaching numbers.

Real world

The National Academies (2024) ask for a twin to be defined at a fidelity that makes it fit for purpose. Advising a schedule and writing a setpoint in an occupied room are different purposes. A twin fit for the first has not shown it is fit for the second until its band has held where the cost is highest: occupied hours.

Self-studyWhy the calculator scores occupied hours only2 min▶

A cold room at 03:00 with nobody in it costs nothing but a slightly longer warm-up. The same error at 09:00 costs comfort. Scoring all hours equally would let the twin's good nights hide its bad mornings. Weight your checks by where the cost lands; for Kittiwake that is 50 occupied hours in a week of 168. Small samples like this also mean one or two hours move a percentage a lot: read the counts, not just the shares.

Part 3 · covers guardrails, fallback and override

Guardrails: limits, a way back, and a person who can always say stop

5 min live

A twin may act only inside limits it can justify: an honest band and un-flagged surprises, the two gates from practitioner session 8. When either fails, it does not argue; it falls back to the last schedule known to be safe. A person can override at any moment. Every automatic action, every fallback and every override goes into the decision log. The figure is the state machine.

LiveFour rules for a twin that acts3 min▶
  • Act only inside limits you can justify. The band must have held on this floor (practitioner session 8 gated each floor; with the retrofit the building covered 92.1 percent while floor 4 covered 69.0) and the band's cold edge must clear the comfort floor.
  • Fall back when the surprises shift or the data stops. Floor 4's innovation shift of z 11.44 after the unlogged retrofit is exactly the trigger. A data dropout is the other: a twin predicting through a gap may advise, never act. Gaps, clocks and dropouts are covered in learn IoT data; better detectors than a mean-shift z live in learn anomaly detection.
  • A person can always override. The override beats the twin, needs no reason in the moment, and is logged. An override pattern (the same floor, every Monday) is itself a signal that the model or the limit is wrong.
  • Log every automatic action. The decision log from practitioner session 1 gets a row per command: the prediction, its band, the model version, the guard's verdict. Without it, nobody can audit what the twin did while nobody was watching.
Self-studyWhat "the last safe schedule" means2 min▶

Fallback is not "turn the twin off and hope". It is a named schedule that ran before, under people, without complaints: for Kittiwake, the normal week-1 heating (21 C on weekday days, 19 C otherwise). It must live outside the twin, in the controller itself, so that a twin crash, a data outage or a model version gone wrong all land in the same place. Write it down, version it like any other configuration, and test the switch to it on a quiet day.

Real world

The AIAA and AIA (2020) position paper follows a twin through As Designed, As Built, As Used and As Maintained. Every maintenance change is a new configuration, and a model fitted to the last one has lost its licence to act until it is checked again. Fallback is what the building does in that gap.

Build-along

Write the guard between a prediction and a command

★ 22 min · everyone builds

You will write a guard function that takes the twin's predicted temperature, its band half-width, the comfort floor and the floor's flag state, and returns ALLOW, ASK or FALL BACK. Then you run it on six toy cases, including a band too narrow to be honest and a flagged floor. The figure is the decision table you will code: order matters.

Decide the order of the checks

Write the four checks from the figure on paper before any code. The flag comes first because a shifted floor makes its band meaningless. The width check comes before the comfort check because a too-narrow band would pass the comfort check for the wrong reason.

the order, in words1. flagged or data stopped -> FALL BACK 2. band narrower than sensor noise -> ASK 3. cold edge of band >= comfort floor -> ALLOW 4. anything else -> ASK

Set the width floor from the sensor

A 95 percent band on a reading can never be narrower than the sensor's own noise: 1.96 x 0.4 C is about 0.78 C of half-width. Kittiwake's honest band is 2.20 C wide on average (half-width 1.10); the trust-the-sensors band is 1.28 C (half-width 0.64), under the floor. These half-widths are the bench canon halved; the six cases below are toy inputs, not runs of the engine.

the width floorSENSOR_HALF = 1.96 * 0.4 # 0.784 C honest band 2.20 C wide -> half 1.10 -> above the floor trust sensors 1.28 C wide -> half 0.64 -> below the floor

Write and run guard.py

Save it as guard.py in a scratch folder and run python3 -I guard.py.

guard.py# A guard between a twin's prediction and an automatic command. SENSOR_HALF = 1.96 * 0.4 # a 95% band can never be narrower than the sensor's own noise def guard(pred_c, half_c, flagged, floor_c=19.0): if flagged: # surprises shifted, or the data stopped return "FALL BACK" if half_c < SENSOR_HALF: # a band this narrow has not been earned return "ASK" if pred_c - half_c >= floor_c: # even the cold edge of the band is comfortable return "ALLOW" return "ASK" # the band reaches below the comfort floor cases = [ ("honest band, warm room", 21.0, 1.10, False), ("honest band, near floor", 19.6, 1.10, False), ("narrow band, near floor", 19.8, 0.64, False), ("trust the sensors fully", 20.4, 0.02, False), ("flagged floor, warm room", 21.0, 1.10, True), ("honest band, cold room", 18.2, 1.10, False), ] for name, pred, half, flagged in cases: lower = pred - half print(f"{name:26s} pred {pred:4.1f} low edge {lower:5.2f} -> {guard(pred, half, flagged)}")
Outputhonest band, warm room pred 21.0 low edge 19.90 -> ALLOW honest band, near floor pred 19.6 low edge 18.50 -> ASK narrow band, near floor pred 19.8 low edge 19.16 -> ASK trust the sensors fully pred 20.4 low edge 20.38 -> ASK flagged floor, warm room pred 21.0 low edge 19.90 -> FALL BACK honest band, cold room pred 18.2 low edge 17.10 -> ASK

Read the two cases that matter

The narrow band near the floor has a cold edge of 19.16 C, above 19: a guard without the width check would have said ALLOW. The flagged floor has a comfortable prediction and an honest-looking band, and still falls back: after a shift, the band describes a building that no longer exists.

try thisDelete the width check, run again, and count how many verdicts change. Then set floor_c=18.0 and run again. Which case flips, and why?

Write the log line

Every call to the guard is a decision log row, whatever it returns. Add one print per case in the format below, with the model version you would attach.

decision_log rowdecision_id, hour, floor_id, command, pred_c, half_c, flagged, verdict, model_version, overridden_by

The habit to keep. Put the guard in the path of every automatic command, check the flag before the band and the band's width before its edge, and log every verdict. A twin that acts without a guard is a twin someone will have to explain.

Check yourself

Three questions

What separates "advise" from "act within limits"?

The model can be identical. What changes is who absorbs the error: a person reading a proposal, or the people in the room.

Why is an overconfident band more dangerous when the twin acts than when it advises?

Trusting the sensors fully leaves the predictions and the energy estimate almost unchanged, but the band shrinks to about 1.28 C and covers 77.4 percent instead of 96.8 (constructed). A person reading advice can be sceptical; a guard cannot.

Which of these should trigger a fallback to the last safe schedule?

A single surprise is noise; a shift in the surprises means the building changed under the model. A data outage means the twin is predicting blind. An override is logged and watched for patterns, but one override is not a fallback.

Take this with you

Cheat sheet

Three closures. Advise (a person decides), approve (a person signs), act within limits (a person can override).
The limits are the contract. Sign off the limits, not the model.
Asymmetry. Wrong cold costs people now; wrong warm costs kWh you can fix next week. Lean warm.
Score where it costs. Check the band in occupied hours, and read counts, not just shares.
Width before edge. A band narrower than 1.96 x the sensor noise never earns ALLOW.
Fallback trigger. Surprises shift (z above 4) or data stops: go to the last safe schedule.
Override and log. A person always wins; every command, verdict and override is a log row.
Constructed. Kittiwake's numbers are generated; your numbers will differ.
