# Official course map - learn-digital-twins-with-phoebe

"Digital Twins", by Phoebe Fu. Bucket emrg, two-track: Leader a1-a6, Practitioner p1-p10 (p10 is the
bench). 45 minutes per session. Skeleton approved by Phoebe 2026-11-24 (public KOL followers, building
thermal twin bench, blueprint cobalt + copper, public repo + Pages).
Internal build document: never linked from an audience page.

Angle: a data/AI course on twins, never a vendor tour. A model that claims to mirror a physical asset is
only worth the evidence that it still does. Owns: purpose and fidelity, physics vs data vs hybrid models,
calibration of model parameters, data assimilation (the Kalman filter), model drift vs asset drift, the
configuration / change log (as-built vs as-designed), what-if runs with honest uncertainty, governance.

Running case: **Kittiwake Hall**, a CONSTRUCTED six-storey teaching building whose facilities team builds a
thermal twin of its floors to plan heating. "Kittiwake" grepped clean across the estate on 2026-11-24.
Every Kittiwake number is generated by the seeded engine `assets/dtw-live.js` and is labelled constructed
wherever it appears. No real company, product, vendor, platform, building or incident appears anywhere.
Research and standards are cited by author/body and year. Not investment advice (landing page and a1 say
so, because savings figures are money).

**Re-verify before delivery.** Fast-moving field: standards editions (ISO 23247 parts, ASHRAE Guideline 14
editions) and the National Academies follow-ups change. Contested: the size of savings attributed to twins,
and when "autonomous" twins that act without people arrive. Both are taught as disagreements; no page gives
a savings percentage from the literature or a date.

## 2. Verified facts

### F1. National Academies 2024 (read at source, nationalacademies.org/read/26894, Summary)
National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (2024), "Foundational Research Gaps and Future
Directions for Digital Twins", The National Academies Press, Washington, DC.
- Definition (adapted from AIAA 2020): a digital twin is a set of virtual information constructs that mimics
  the structure, context, and behavior of a natural, engineered, or social system (or system-of-systems), is
  dynamically updated with data from its physical twin, has a predictive capability, and informs decisions
  that realize value.
- "The bidirectional interaction between the virtual and the physical is central to the digital twin."
  Conclusion 2-1: the interaction forms a feedback loop that comprises dynamic data-driven model updating.
- Finding 2-1: "A digital twin is more than just simulation and modeling."
- Conclusion 3-1: a digital twin should be defined at a level of fidelity and resolution that makes it
  **fit for purpose**.
- VVUQ (verification, validation and uncertainty quantification) is "an area of particular need";
  Conclusion 2-2: digital twins require VVUQ to be a **continual** process that must adapt to changes in the
  physical counterpart.
Used on: a1, a2, a4, p1, p7, p8, index. Short quotes only (under 15 words each, one per page).

### F2. AIAA and AIA position paper 2020 (read at source, PDF)
AIAA Digital Engineering Integration Committee (2020), "Digital Twin: Definition & Value", an AIAA and AIA
Position Paper, December 2020.
- Definition: a set of virtual information constructs that mimics the structure, context and behavior of an
  individual / unique physical asset, or a group of physical assets, is dynamically updated with data from its
  physical twin throughout its life cycle and informs decisions that realize value.
- Essential elements: a virtual representation (model), a physical realization (asset), and a transfer of
  data / information (connected) between the two. "Hence to have a Digital Twin requires a physical asset."
- Life-cycle phases named: "As Designed", "As Built", "As Used" and "As Maintained".
- Models "can be purely data-driven, purely" physics-based or hybrid (the paper lists the kinds; pages
  paraphrase).
Used on: a1, a3, p1, p2, p6.

### F3. Origin of the concept (Grieves; read at source as bibliographic record + excerpt; naming contested)
Grieves, M. and Vickers, J. (2017), "Digital Twin: Mitigating Unpredictable, Undesirable Emergent Behavior in
Complex Systems", in Kahlen, Flumerfelt, Alves (eds.), Transdisciplinary Perspectives on Complex Systems,
Springer, doi 10.1007/978-3-319-38756-7_4.
- The chapter dates the model to **2002**, presented for the formation of a product lifecycle management
  (PLM) centre, on a slide called "Conceptual Ideal for PLM"; the model had no name then.
- Grieves later dated an earlier 2002 presentation (October, at a manufacturing management forum) than the
  one he first cited (reported, secondary).
- **Contested:** who named it and whether the concept began there. One secondary account says the term
  was suggested by Vickers around 2010; a published critique argues the 2002 claim lacks contemporaneous
  documentary evidence. Teach: "the usual origin story, and the dispute about it". No page settles it.
Used on: a1 (one card), p1 (one line).

### F4. Kalman 1960 (read at source as bibliographic record)
Kalman, R. E. (1960), "A New Approach to Linear Filtering and Prediction Problems", Transactions of the
ASME, Journal of Basic Engineering 82(1): 35-45, doi 10.1115/1.3662552.
- Reformulates filtering and prediction in state-space (state-transition) form; the error covariance obeys
  its own recursion. Pages teach the standard predict / update form; the closed form for a random walk is
  derived and checked in the engine's selfTest (gain 0.5198 for q = 0.09, r = 0.16).
Used on: p4, p10, a1 (one line).

### F5. ASHRAE Guideline 14 calibration criteria (REPORTED; paywalled)
ASHRAE Guideline 14, "Measurement of Energy, Demand, and Water Savings" (editions 2002 and 2014).
Reported via secondary papers (NREL 2018; a 2020 SimBuild paper): a model is commonly treated as calibrated
when **hourly CV(RMSE) is at most 30 percent and NMBE within plus or minus 10 percent**; **monthly 15 and 5
percent**. The same thresholds are reported in FEMP and IPMVP protocols. Garrett and New (2016, ASHRAE
Transactions) question whether these metrics are sufficient for calibration (reported). Pages say
"reported", name the edition caveat, and note that denominators (n vs n - p) and sign conventions differ.
Used on: p3, a4.

### F6. ISO 23247 (REPORTED; paywalled; table of contents read)
ISO 23247-1:2021, "Automation systems and integration - Digital twin framework for manufacturing - Part 1:
Overview and general principles", published 2021-10, ISO/TC 184/SC 4. The free preview's contents list
"observable manufacturing elements" (clause 4.4), limitations and boundaries, requirements, and a high-level
outline for implementations. We did not read the clauses. Pages say it exists, its scope is manufacturing,
and that it frames a twin around observable elements; no requirement is quoted.
Used on: a2, p8.

### F7. Grey-box building models (Bacher and Madsen 2011; read at source as bibliographic record, method reported)
Bacher, P. and Madsen, H. (2011), "Identifying suitable models for the heat dynamics of buildings", Energy
and Buildings 43(7): 1511-1522, doi 10.1016/j.enbuild.2011.02.005.
- Grey-box models: continuous-time stochastic differential equations (physics structure, parameters from
  data). A forward-selection procedure grows model complexity (more RC nodes) and compares models with
  likelihood-ratio tests; applied to a test building (reported via secondary summaries).
Used on: p2, p3, a2.

## 3. The bench canon (CONSTRUCTED; engine `assets/dtw-live.js`, checked by `materials/dtw_reference.py`)

**Agreement:** `node materials/dtw_canon.js . materials/dtw_canon.json` then
`python3 -I materials/dtw_reference.py --compare materials/dtw_canon.json` prints
"AGREE ... 2816 values" (4 seeds; ladder, anti-lever, break and 8 custom runs each; 1e-9 relative). With
`--plant 0.001` it prints DISAGREE (2744 values) and exits 1: the comparator can fail.
**selfTest:** 6 checks pass on load; each of the 5 planted faults (gainSign, noCovUpdate, calibStale,
bandNoR, breakLogged) makes at least one check fail.

World (declared on the widget): 6 floors, 14 days hourly, day 0 a Monday. Week 1 normal schedule (21 C from
7 to 19 on weekdays, 19 C otherwise); week 2 the real building runs a night setback (15 C at night and
weekends). Weather random walk from 6 C, 3.5 C daily swing; solar per day; occupants 2.5 kW per floor in
occupied hours x seeded 0.6 to 1.0 (the twin assumes 0.8). As-built gap: real floors lose heat kU = 1.15 to
1.45 times the drawings and hold kC = 0.70 to 0.95 of the designed mass. Thermostat: 4 kW per degree below
setpoint, up to 20 kW, reading its own sensor. Sensor noise sd 0.4 C; unmodelled disturbance sd 0.12 C/h.
Twin: 2-node RC (room air, building mass), Ci 6 kWh/K, Cm 20 kWh/K x kC, Rim 0.5, Rio 10 / kU, Rmo 4 / kU
(K/kW); end floors (1 and 6) lose 1.3 times more in the drawings too.

- Raw sensors alone: RMSE 0.404 C against the true room.
- Rung 4 what-if (24 h ahead from each midnight of week 2, night setback, 95% band): **coverage 96.8%**,
  mean band width **2.20 C**. Per floor coverage 95.2, 94.0, 98.8, 97.6, 99.4, 95.8 percent.
- Kalman gain on the room reading, week 2 mean: 0.445 to 0.448 per floor.
- Calibration (grid search, week 1): kU found 1.50, 1.20, 1.26, 1.42, 1.42, 1.28 against true 1.42, 1.19,
  1.21, 1.41, 1.41, 1.21 (all within 0.08). kC found 1.62, 1.00, 1.68, 0.90, 0.80, 1.20 against true 0.85,
  0.72, 0.89, 0.88, 0.90, 0.76: **one week pins down heat loss, not thermal mass** (an identifiability
  lesson; p3 teaches it). Calibration RMSE against sensors 0.460 to 0.526 C.
- Design twin per floor: RMSE 2.35 to 5.81 C; energy error -21.6 to -42.5 percent. Calibrated per floor
  energy error -1.2 to +3.5 percent.
- **Anti-lever** (assumed sensor sd 0.01 C, "trust the sensors fully", gain 0.999): RMSE **0.403 C** (the
  sensors' own 0.404: the twin copies the noise), band width **1.28 C**, coverage **77.4%**. Energy estimate
  unchanged (+1.7%): trusting sensors does not fix or break parameters.
- Assumed sensor sd ladder (custom runs, clean): 0.4 -> RMSE 0.218, cover 96.8%, width 2.20; 0.2 -> 0.297,
  88.5%, 1.56; 0.1 -> 0.365, 80.7%, 1.35; 0.01 -> 0.403, 77.4%, 1.28.
- **Break** (unlogged insulation retrofit, floor 4, start of week 2, kU x 0.6): floor 4 flagged alone
  (innovation shift z = 11.44; mean innovation week 1 +0.015 C, week 2 +0.638 C; other floors |z| below
  0.7). Floor 4 energy estimate error **+63.1%**, whole building **+8.9%** (5,153 vs 4,732 kWh). Floor 4
  coverage 69.0%, building coverage 92.1%. Tracking RMSE 0.258 C overall, 0.399 C on floor 4 (the filter
  still follows the sensors; the innovations carry the warning).
- Break with the anti-lever (0.01, break): coverage 69.7%, floor 4 still flagged.

## 4. Per-session coverage

Leader (no code):
- a1 A twin is a promise to stay in sync - F1 definition, F2 elements, F3 origin and dispute; the sync loop;
  Kittiwake intro; bench headline numbers (design 4.45 C / -32.9%, Kalman 0.218 C, break +63.1% on floor 4).
  Not investment advice.
- a2 Fidelity is a choice, not a virtue - F1 fit for purpose; F7 grey-box; resolution vs decision; cost of
  fidelity; ISO 23247 scope (F6 reported). Kittiwake: a 2-node model per floor is enough to plan heating,
  not to place a radiator.
- a3 The as-built gap - F2 As Designed / As Built / As Used / As Maintained; canon kU 1.15 to 1.45 vs drawings;
  design twin -32.9% energy; drawings as a prior, measurements as evidence.
- a4 Reading a digital twin claim - 3D render vs live model, "real-time", savings figures without a baseline
  or band; F5 calibration criteria reported; contested savings (teach the disagreement, no figures).
- a5 Why the warehouse playbook breaks here - source (a physical asset that changes), identity (which
  configuration), ground truth (only sensors, noisy), feedback loop (the twin's advice changes the building),
  cost of being wrong (comfort, energy, safety).
- a6 From warehouse to digital twins: the transfer map - carries over / must learn / must unlearn; 90-day
  self-study ladder (official/free: National Academies report free to read online, AIAA/AIA paper free PDF,
  Kalman 1960 paper, open course notes on state estimation).
Practitioner:
- p1 Anatomy of a twin - asset, model, data link, decisions; F1, F2; Kittiwake's four parts and the hourly
  record (weather, solar, timetable, heat meter, sensor).
- p2 Physics, data or both - white / black / grey box; F7; RC network drawn; what each needs.
- p3 Calibrating a model to measurements - grid search on week 1; kU recovered, kC not (identifiability);
  F5 reported criteria; overfitting the calibration week.
- p4 Keeping state in sync: the Kalman filter by hand - predict, update, gain; F4; the random-walk closed
  form 0.5198; canon gain 0.445.
- p5 When the model drifts and when the asset does - innovations; break canon z 11.44; floor 4 only.
- p6 The change log is a data source - configuration as data; F2 As Maintained; unlogged retrofit +63.1%.
- p7 What-if runs with honest bands - band from covariance + sensor noise; coverage 96.8% vs 77.4%;
  saving 21.5% vs true 20.1%; design twin 23.9%.
- p8 Twin governance - versions, owners, who may act; F1 continual VVUQ; F6 reported.
- p9 Closing the loop - advice vs control; cost of being wrong in a real building; contested "autonomous
  twin" timelines (no date).
- p10 The twin sync bench - the whole ladder computed live; anti-lever; break.

  below the 21 C setpoint: 18.6 C on average over six floors in occupied hours of the setback week (true room) (p9 teaches this: a 19 C guardrail
  refuses every automatic command, so the twin can only advise). p10 says so in Part 0.

  canon numbers are unaffected because they are what the engine prints.
